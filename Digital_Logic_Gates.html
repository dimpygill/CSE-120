Digital Logic Gates

Learn how simple rules turn 0s and 1s into decisions. No prior electrical circuit knowledge is required.

1. What is a logic gate?

A logic gate is a small building block of a digital system. It receives one or more inputs, each represented by 0 or 1, follows a simple rule, and produces an output of 0 or 1.

Example: A garden sprinkler should run only when the soil is dry and the system is enabled. A logic gate can express that decision without requiring us to study the electronic components inside it.

Inputs are the facts the system checks. The output is its decision. A truth table lists every possible input combination and the corresponding output.

For two inputs, there are 4 combinations (00, 01, 10, 11). For three inputs, there are 8. A NOT gate uses only one input.

Why are gates important?

Gates are used in calculators, computer processors, memory systems, security devices, and automated controls. Complex digital systems combine many gates to make more complicated decisions.

10. Questions with worked answers

Question A: What is the difference between OR and XOR?

Answer: OR produces 1 if at least one input is 1, including when both are 1. XOR produces 1 only when the inputs differ. For A=1 and B=1, OR=1 but XOR=0.

Question B: How is NAND related to AND?

Answer: NAND reverses AND's output. AND gives 1 only when both inputs are 1. Therefore NAND gives 0 only when both inputs are 1.

Question C: How is NOR related to OR?

Answer: NOR reverses OR's output. OR gives 0 only when both inputs are 0, so NOR gives 1 only in that case.

Question D: A fan runs only when three conditions are true. Which gate rule fits?

Answer: A three-input AND rule. The output is 1 only for 111; every other combination produces 0.

Question E: Why does NOT have one input?

Answer: NOT simply reverses one logical value: 0 becomes 1 and 1 becomes 0. It does not compare two conditions.

11. Key takeaways

AND: all true. OR: at least one true. NOT: reverse. NAND: not all true. NOR: none true. XOR: different inputs.

To solve a logic problem, first define what 0 and 1 mean, then identify the rule, and finally fill in the truth table one row at a time.
