Self-Assessment Activity: Thinking Styles

Purpose: Explore which thinking styles feel most natural to you: Analyzer, Manager, Partner, or Visionary.

Name: __________________________

Date: __________________

This is an informal reflection activity. It describes your current preferences; it does not measure ability or assign a permanent personality type. You may recognize strengths in several styles.

1. Understand the Four Styles

StyleFocus
A — AnalyzerFacts, evidence, logical reasoning, and checking accuracy.
M — ManagerPlanning, organizing tasks, tracking details, and managing time.
P — PartnerListening, including others, understanding needs, and supporting cooperation.
V — VisionaryNew ideas, possibilities, creative alternatives, and the big picture.

2. Rank Your Preferences

  1. Complete this activity individually. Think about how you usually approach everyday tasks.
  2. For each question, rank all four options: 4 = most like you; 3 = next most like you; 2 = less like you; 1 = least like you.
  3. Use each number exactly once within each question. There are no right or wrong answers.
  4. Write the rank in the blank beside each option.

Question 1. When starting a project, I prefer to:

A. _____ Examine the facts and information available.
M. _____ Create a plan and schedule.
P. _____ Discuss everyone's interests and needs.
V. _____ Explore the big picture and possible new ideas.

Question 2. When learning something new, I prefer to:

A. _____ Understand the reasoning and evidence behind it.
M. _____ Follow clear instructions and practice step by step.
P. _____ Discuss it with others and learn through conversation.
V. _____ Imagine new ways to use it.

Question 3. When solving a problem, I usually:

A. _____ Compare information and check which solution makes sense.
M. _____ Break the problem into manageable steps.
P. _____ Consider how the solution affects other people.
V. _____ Suggest a different or unexpected approach.

Question 4. When planning an event, I naturally focus on:

A. _____ Comparing costs and checking practical facts.
M. _____ Organizing the schedule and responsibilities.
P. _____ Making sure everyone's preferences are considered.
V. _____ Suggesting an original theme or experience.

Question 5. After completing a task, I tend to:

A. _____ Check the result and look for errors.
M. _____ Confirm that all required steps were completed.
P. _____ Ask others about their experience and share feedback.
V. _____ Think of improvements or other possibilities.

5. Other Notes: Additional Strengths

Your results may not capture every skill you have. If you recognize additional strengths, including those associated with a lower-scoring style, describe them here. You can also explain any result that does not fit your experience.

For each additional strength you choose to include, write:

  1. The skill or thinking style you also recognize in yourself.
  2. A specific example of when you demonstrated it.
  3. A responsibility you feel capable of handling with that skill.

Example: “My highest score is Manager, and I enjoy organizing tasks. I also have Analyzer skills: when planning a family outing, I compared prices and checked the total against our budget. I feel capable of checking costs and verifying whether a plan meets its requirements.”

My additional strengths, examples, and responsibilities:

If you have no additional notes at this time, write “No additional notes at this time.” Your highest-scoring style does not limit the responsibilities you can take on.

6. Submit Your Individual Response

Submit one document containing your name, all five ranked responses, the four totals, your profile reflection, and your Other Notes response.

Completion check: Each question uses ranks 1–4 once; all totals are included; your reflection gives a specific example; and the Other Notes section is completed.

Points: There are no right or wrong answers. Upload your completed response to receive the full 1 point. Your thinking style and scores are not graded.
