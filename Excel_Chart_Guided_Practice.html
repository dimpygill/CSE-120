In-Class Guided Practice: Excel Team Roles Chart

Goal: Create an Excel chart with thinking roles along the horizontal axis so you can compare team members within each role.

Full credit for uploading: Upload your Excel workbook to receive full points. This is guided practice. Chart mistakes or different role choices will not reduce your points.

Work with your team to collect scores, but each person creates and uploads an Excel file. The visuals below use sample names and scores. Replace them with your team’s names and actual self-assessment totals.

Step 1. Create the score table in Excel

  1. Open Excel and choose Blank workbook.
  2. Click A1 and enter Thinking Mode.
  3. In B1, C1, D1, and E1, enter your four teammates’ names.
  4. In A2:A5, enter Analyzer, Manager, Partner, and Visionary.
  5. Enter each person’s corresponding scores underneath their name in B2:E5.
  6. If a heading does not fit, double-click the right edge of its column letter to widen the column.

Step 2. Select the whole table

  1. Click A1.
  2. Hold the mouse button and drag to E5. Release the button.
  3. Check that the selected area includes the names, role labels, and every score.

Alternative: Click the Name Box beside the formula bar, type A1:E5, and press Enter.

Step 3. Insert a Clustered Column chart

  1. Keep A1:E5 selected.
  2. Click the Insert tab.
  3. In the Charts group, select Insert Column or Bar Chart.
  4. Under 2-D Column, choose Clustered Column, usually the first option.

Step 4. Put the roles on the horizontal axis

  1. Look at the labels along the bottom of the chart.
  2. They should be Analyzer, Manager, Partner, and Visionary.
  3. If the bottom shows people’s names, click the chart, then select Chart Design → Switch Row/Column.
  4. Check that the legend shows the teammates’ names.

Step 5. Add a title and show the scores

  1. Click the chart title and replace it with Team Thinking Preferences by Role.
  2. With the chart selected, choose Chart Design → Add Chart Element → Data Labels → Outside End.
  3. If the legend is missing, choose Add Chart Element → Legend → Bottom.
  4. Drag a chart corner to enlarge it until names and values are readable.

Your finished chart should look similar to this:

This example uses the sample scores from Step 1. Your chart will have your team’s names and scores. Colors and styling may differ.

How to read it: Choose one role, compare the four columns in that group, and use the legend to identify the person with the tallest column. Equal heights indicate a tie.

Step 6. Record possible roles and explain your choices

  1. Move the chart to the right of the score table by dragging its outer border, leaving cells below the table available.
  2. In A8:C8, enter Role, Suggested member, and Reason.
  3. In A9:A12, enter the four roles.
  4. Use your chart to identify the highest scores and any ties.
  5. Discuss interests, additional strengths, and willingness. Agree who could practice each role.
  6. Fill in B9:C12 with names and brief reasons.

If several people want the same role: Compare second preferences and Other Notes, ask for volunteers, and agree how to cover all four perspectives. Scores suggest preferences; the group makes the role choices.

Step 7. Save and upload your workbook

  1. Save the file as FirstName_LastName_TeamRoles.xlsx.
  2. Use Excel Workbook (.xlsx) as the file type. Excel for the web users can download a copy through the File menu.
  3. Keep the score table, chart, and role-selection table in the workbook.
  4. Open this assignment in Canvas, choose the file-upload option, select your workbook, and submit it.
  5. Each person uploads their own workbook. Uploading your practice work earns full points.
Before uploading: Check that your file opens, your chart shows roles on the horizontal axis, and your teammates’ names appear in the legend. Submit the .xlsx workbook rather than only a screenshot.
