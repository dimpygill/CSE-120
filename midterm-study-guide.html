🔁 Loops: for & while
for loop — use when you know how many times to repeat, or when iterating over a sequence.
while loop — use when repeating until a condition changes (user input, sentinel value, etc.).
# for loop — range(start, stop, step)
for i in range(1, 6):
    print(i)           # prints 1 2 3 4 5

# for loop over a list
grades = [85, 92, 78]
for g in grades:
    print(g)

# while loop — sentinel pattern
total = 0
count = 0
hours = float(input("Hours (or -1 to stop): "))
while hours != -1:
    total += hours
    count += 1
    hours = float(input("Hours (or -1 to stop): "))
print("Total:", total)
for loopwhile loop
Use whenCount/sequence is knownCondition drives stopping
RiskOff-by-one errorsInfinite loop if condition never changes
Common patternrange(n), iterating a listSentinel value, user input
⏭️ break & continue
break — exits the loop immediately, regardless of the loop condition.
continue — skips the rest of the current iteration and jumps back to the loop condition.
# break — stop when we hit -1
while True:
    hours = float(input("Hours (-1 to quit): "))
    if hours == -1:
        break              # exits the while loop
    print("Recorded:", hours)

# continue — skip 0 (session skipped)
sessions = [2, 0, 3.5, 0, 1]
for h in sessions:
    if h == 0:
        print("Session skipped")
        continue           # skip rest; go to next h
    print("Valid session:", h)
💡 Memory trick: break = breaks out of the loop entirely. continue = continues to the next round, skipping this one.

Chapter 2 🤝 Teamwork & Communication
🧠 Four Thinking Styles (HBDM)
StyleFocusTheir question
A — AnalyzerFacts, data, logic, accuracy"Where's the evidence?"
M — ManagerPlans, schedules, step-by-step"What's the checklist?"
P — PartnerPeople, relationships, feelings"How does this affect everyone?"
V — VisionaryNew ideas, big picture, creativity"What if we tried something new?"
Area of Agreement — where two teammates think alike (easy to agree, but fewer new ideas).
Area of Contribution — where teammates think differently (creative friction → better decisions).
💡 Strong teams need all four styles. A team missing one style has a predictable blind spot.

How to discover thinking styles: preference ranking activity (one technique), observation over time, reflection after conflict, watching role performance, direct conversation.

🏗️ 5 Steps to Build an Effective Team
  1. Select complementary members — cover all thinking styles; avoid blind spots
  2. Develop a shared vision — everyone agrees on what "success" looks like
  3. Agree on a shared objective — specific, measurable goals and timeline (the concrete "what" and "by when")
  4. Create an execution plan — assign clear roles, responsibilities, deadlines
  5. Evaluate and adjust — regularly check progress; update the plan as needed
📈 Tuckman's 4 Stages of Team Development
⚪ Forming 🔴 Storming 🔵 Norming 🟢 Performing
StageWhat's happeningKey point
FormingPolite, cautious, getting to know each other; productivity lowEveryone is figuring out roles and norms
StormingConflict and disagreement; members test limitsNormal and necessary — don't skip it
NormingRoles are clear, trust builds, productivity risesMost teams operate here
PerformingPeak teamwork — high trust, seamless collaborationRare; takes time and intentional effort
⚠️ Conflict in Storming is not failure — teams that skip conflict never learn to resolve it and stay fragile.
📡 Communication
Key model: Sender → Message → Filters → Receiver.
Filters = receiver's background, assumptions, and emotions. Same words mean different things to different people.

Two layers of communication:

  • Verbal / written — the actual words
  • Non-verbal — tone, body language, facial expressions (often carries more meaning than words)
Audience styleFrame your message as…
AnalyzerData, evidence, logical comparison — keep emotion out
ManagerDetailed plan, proven approach, covers risks
PartnerHow it helps people, shared values, personal and empathetic
VisionaryNew, exciting, forward-looking — emphasize what's innovative
💡 Presentation Rule of Three: Tell them what you'll tell them → Tell them → Tell them what you told them.

Technical Report sections: Cover/Executive Summary → Problem Definition → Research → Analysis → Recommendation → Issues/Dependencies. The Executive Summary is the most-read section.

🤜🤛 Three Types of Negotiation

😔 Soft

One side gives in. Short-term peace but long-term resentment.

💥 Hard

Both sides dig in. "Win" matters more than the best outcome.

🌟 Win-Win (Principled)

Decisions based on merit and shared criteria. Best outcome for everyone. This is the goal.

Chapter 3 💡 Creative Problem Solving
🔄 The CPS Process
1
Identify & Define the Problem — Clearly state what you're solving. A well-defined problem is already half solved. What's in scope? What are the constraints?
2
Gather Information — Research facts, collect data, ask questions. Don't jump to solutions yet.
3
Generate Ideas (Brainstorm) — Quantity over quality at first. Defer judgment. Wild ideas are welcome. Build on others' ideas.
4
Evaluate & Select a Solution — Apply criteria (cost, time, feasibility). Compare against requirements, not personal preference.
5
Implement — Put the plan into action. Assign roles, set timeline, execute.
6
Evaluate & Reflect — Did it work? What would you do differently? Honest reflection turns one project into learning for the next.
Key CPS principle: Separate divergent thinking (generating many ideas — no judgment) from convergent thinking (narrowing down — apply criteria). Mixing them too early kills creativity.
💡 The biggest CPS mistake: jumping to Step 4 before Steps 2 and 3 are complete. Solutions built on incomplete information usually fail.

Practice 🧪 Practice Questions & Answers

Click a question to reveal the answer. Try to answer it first before clicking.

1. What is the output of: print(type(3.14))? ▶
<class 'float'> — 3.14 is a decimal number, so Python assigns it the float type.
2. What does int("42") do? Why would you need it? ▶
It converts the string "42" into the integer 42. You need it because input() always returns a string — to do math on user input, you must convert it first.
3. A student enters 2.5 hours. Which category does it fall in, and what code produces that? ▶
Moderate Session (≥1 and <3).
if hours >= 3:
    print("Long Session")
elif hours >= 1:
    print("Moderate Session")
elif hours > 0:
    print("Short Session")
Python checks conditions top to bottom and runs the first True one.
4. What is the difference between break and continue? ▶
break exits the loop entirely — no more iterations run.
continue skips the rest of the current iteration and jumps back to the loop condition — the loop keeps going.
5. How many times does this loop print? for i in range(2, 8, 2): print(i) ▶
3 times — it prints 2, 4, 6. range(2, 8, 2) starts at 2, stops before 8, steps by 2.
6. When would you choose a while loop over a for loop? ▶
Use a while loop when the number of repetitions is unknown and depends on a condition — for example, repeating until the user enters -1, or until a calculation reaches a target.
7. A teammate always asks "Where's the data?" before agreeing to anything. What is their dominant thinking style? ▶
Analyzer (A) — Analyzers rely on facts, evidence, and logical reasoning before making decisions.
8. What is the "Area of Contribution" in a team, and why does it matter? ▶
The Area of Contribution is where teammates think differently from each other. It matters because the contrast in perspectives generates creative ideas and catches blind spots — same-style teams agree easily but produce fewer breakthroughs.
9. A team is working smoothly, roles are clear, and they deliver consistently — but aren't yet exceptional. Which Tuckman stage are they in? ▶
🔵 Norming — Trust is built, norms are established, productivity is solid. Performing is the next stage, but it's rarer and takes more time to reach.
10. Why is Storming considered necessary, not a failure? ▶
Storming forces a team to surface disagreements and learn how to resolve them. Teams that skip conflict never develop the skills to handle it — they remain fragile. Working through Storming builds the trust and norms that Norming and Performing depend on.
11. You need to convince an Analyzer and a Visionary to support the same idea. How do you frame it differently? ▶
Analyzer: "I compared three options and the data shows this one reduces errors by 40% while adding only 20 minutes."
Visionary: "This is a new approach nobody on our team has tried — it opens up possibilities we haven't explored yet."
Same idea, different framing based on what each style values.
12. What makes Win-Win negotiation different from Hard negotiation? ▶
In Hard negotiation, both sides dig in and "winning" matters more than the outcome — the result is often deadlock or a bad decision. In Win-Win (Principled) negotiation, decisions are made on shared criteria and merit, with the goal of maximizing benefit for both parties. It requires honesty but produces the best long-term results.
13. Name the two phases of Creative Problem Solving and why they must be kept separate. ▶
Divergent thinking — generating many ideas without judgment (brainstorming). Convergent thinking — evaluating and narrowing down using criteria. They must be kept separate because judging ideas too early suppresses creativity; mixing them causes the team to reject good ideas before fully exploring them.
14. What is a sentinel value in a loop, and how is it used? ▶
A sentinel value is a special input that signals the loop to stop — like entering -1 to quit. The loop checks for it each iteration and uses break or a while condition to exit when it's entered. It lets the program keep collecting input without knowing in advance how many items there will be.
